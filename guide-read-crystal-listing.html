How to read a crystal listing

The vocabulary · The omissions · The glass

Crystal listings have their own vocabulary, and a good deal of it is doing less work than it appears to. Some words are meaningful, some are legally meaningless, and a few reliably signal that the item is not a stone at all. Here is how to read one.

Words that sound like guarantees and are not

  • "Natural" has no fixed definition in this trade. It usually means mined rather than synthetic — but heated, irradiated and dyed material is very often still described as natural, on the reasoning that the raw stone came out of the ground.
  • "Genuine" and "real" mean it is the species named. They say nothing about treatment.
  • "One of a kind" is true of every natural stone ever cut. It is not a quality claim.
  • "High grade", "top quality", "AAA" — seller-defined. See our guide to what AAA actually means.
  • "Hand-sourced", "hand-picked" can be genuinely informative, because it implies the seller saw the material. It is also unverifiable, so weigh it against how much specific detail the rest of the listing carries.

Words that tell you something real

These are technical terms with actual meanings, and their presence is usually a sign of a careful seller:

  • Doubly terminated — pointed at both ends, grown unattached.
  • Phantom — a visible earlier crystal outline inside the stone, from an interruption in growth.
  • Enhydro — contains a sealed pocket of fluid.
  • Druzy — coated in a layer of tiny secondary crystals.
  • Chatoyant — shows a moving band of light, as in tiger's eye.
  • Stabilised — porous material impregnated with resin for durability. Standard practice for much turquoise, and legitimate when disclosed.
  • Reconstituted — ground stone bound back together with resin. Much less than it sounds.
  • Doublet / triplet — a thin slice of stone glued to a backing, and in a triplet capped with a clear dome. Common with opal. Not a solid stone.
Names that mean glass or lab-made

Goldstone and blue goldstone are glass with copper or cobalt inclusions. Opalite is glass. Cherry quartz and strawberry obsidian are usually glass. Aura quartz — angel aura, titanium aura, flame aura — is real quartz with a metal vapour bonded to the surface in a vacuum chamber, so the stone is natural and the colour is manufactured. None of these are frauds when named honestly. They are only a problem when sold as something they are not.

What the omissions tell you

What a listing leaves out is usually more informative than what it includes:

  • No dimensions or weight. The most common and most consequential omission. Photographs have no scale, and "large" is not a measurement.
  • No locality. A specific origin is a point of pride for someone who knows it. Vagueness often means the seller bought from a wholesaler and does not know.
  • No treatment statement. On species where treatment is routine — citrine, blue topaz, turquoise, agate — silence is not reassurance.
  • A stock photograph on a one-of-a-kind item. If a listing says the piece is unique but the image is clearly reused, you do not know what will arrive.

Reading the photographs

Assume every photograph is the most flattering one available, then look for specific things. Wet stones look more saturated than dry ones, so a piece photographed with visible water droplets will arrive paler. Strong backlighting hides cloudiness. A single angle hides whatever is on the other side. Multiple angles under ordinary light, including one shot with something for scale, is the sign of a seller with nothing to hide.

For anything with play-of-colour or flash — opal, labradorite, moonstone — a still image is close to useless, because the entire effect is what happens when the stone moves. A short video is worth far more than a dozen photographs.

A short checklist

  1. Species named, and locality named specifically.
  2. Actual dimensions and weight given.
  3. Treatment disclosed, or explicitly stated as untreated.
  4. Photographs of the exact piece, from more than one angle.
  5. Flaws mentioned. A seller who names the chip is telling you about everything else too.

Anything that satisfies all five is being sold by someone who handled it. That is worth more than any grade.
